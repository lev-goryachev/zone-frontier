# Zone Frontier — Vision Document v0.1

## Project Overview

**Zone Frontier** is a large-scale strategic gameplay layer for S.T.A.L.K.E.R. GAMMA 0.9.5.

The mod transforms the Zone from a mostly static survival sandbox into a living geopolitical environment where factions fight for:

- territory;
- resources;
- logistics;
- influence;
- manpower;
- production;
- political dominance.

The core gameplay fantasy is:

> "The Zone is no longer just a place where lone stalkers survive.
> It becomes a place where human groups evolve into competing powers struggling for influence, resources, and survival."

Zone Frontier is not intended to replace GAMMA.
Instead, it acts as a macro-strategic layer on top of GAMMA’s survival and combat systems.

---

# High Concept

## Core Fantasy

The player experiences the Zone not only as a lone stalker, but as a participant in a larger political and economic struggle.

The player can:

- rise through the ranks of an existing faction;
- create and grow an independent faction;
- capture and develop territory;
- organize logistics and trade;
- manage manpower;
- establish alliances;
- sabotage enemies;
- wage open warfare;
- economically dominate rival factions.

The long-term objective is:

> Expand and maintain influence across the Zone.

---

# Product Pillars

## 1. Territory Is Power

Territory ownership is the foundation of progression.

Smart terrains become:

- resource nodes;
- outposts;
- defensive positions;
- production hubs;
- logistical checkpoints.

Controlling territory provides:

- access to resources;
- safer logistics;
- stronger economy;
- strategic positioning.

---

## 2. Resources Drive Conflict

Wars are caused by resource pressure, not random hostility.

Factions compete for:

- metal;
- fuel;
- food;
- water;
- production equipment;
- manpower;
- strategic locations.

Economic pressure can be as effective as military force.

---

## 3. People Are Expensive

Human lives are valuable.

Veteran fighters, medics, engineers, and traders represent long-term investments.

Losing trained personnel should be painful.

The optimal strategy is not endless frontal warfare.

Instead, players are encouraged to:

- weaken enemies economically;
- sabotage logistics;
- manipulate diplomacy;
- isolate territories;
- use asymmetric tactics.

---

## 4. Logistics Is Gameplay

Logistics is a core mechanic.

Players must:

- establish supply routes;
- protect caravans;
- maintain food and ammunition flow;
- secure fuel supplies;
- manage storage;
- monitor strategic bottlenecks.

Caravans can:

- be ambushed;
- require escorts;
- become stranded during emissions;
- trigger dynamic combat situations.

---

## 5. The Player Is Not Special

The player follows the same systemic rules as all other factions.

The world continues evolving without player input.

Factions:

- expand;
- collapse;
- attack;
- negotiate;
- starve;
- lose manpower;
- change political alignment.

The player participates in the simulation rather than controlling it externally.

---

## 6. War Is One Tool, Not The Only Tool

Military conquest is not the only path to victory.

Alternative strategic approaches include:

- diplomacy;
- trade;
- economic domination;
- proxy warfare;
- sabotage;
- smuggling;
- espionage;
- mercenary operations.

The strongest faction is not necessarily the faction with the strongest army.

---

## 7. Build To Hold, Not To Decorate

Construction exists to support gameplay.

Structures provide:

- defense;
- storage;
- production;
- maintenance;
- medical support;
- electricity;
- logistics.

The project does not aim to become a decorative sandbox.

Visual construction exists primarily to support:

- tactical gameplay;
- strategic progression;
- territory identity.

---

## 8. GAMMA Handles Survival — Zone Frontier Handles Strategy

GAMMA remains responsible for:

- combat;
- weapons;
- medical systems;
- crafting;
- survival mechanics;
- atmosphere;
- exploration.

Zone Frontier adds:

- strategic simulation;
- faction warfare;
- logistics;
- settlement systems;
- territorial control;
- economy;
- political simulation.

---

# Target Experience

## Early Game

The player starts as:

- a rookie within an existing faction;
OR
- the founder of a tiny independent faction.

Gameplay focuses on:

- survival;
- learning faction systems;
- local defense;
- first supply chains;
- small-scale construction;
- early territorial conflicts.

---

## Mid Game

The player begins:

- capturing resource nodes;
- establishing logistics;
- developing specialized settlements;
- negotiating alliances;
- raiding enemy supply lines;
- investing in infrastructure.

Faction-level gameplay emerges.

---

## Late Game

The Zone evolves into a large-scale strategic conflict.

Gameplay focuses on:

- regional influence;
- advanced logistics;
- political maneuvering;
- proxy warfare;
- economic warfare;
- strategic defense;
- long-term faction survival.

The player can no longer solve problems alone through direct combat.

---

# Strategic Simulation

## Core World Entities

### Resource Points

Large strategic locations responsible for:

- production;
- processing;
- industrial capability;
- resource extraction.

Examples:

- factories;
- hangars;
- workshops;
- laboratories;
- industrial zones.

---

### Outposts

Smaller support locations responsible for:

- logistics;
- caravan support;
- local defense;
- route control;
- territorial influence.

Examples:

- checkpoints;
- camps;
- ruins;
- fortified firesites.

---

# Construction Philosophy

Construction is:

- restricted to predefined smart terrain zones;
- tactical rather than decorative;
- integrated into strategic systems.

Players can build:

- walls;
- barricades;
- storage;
- workshops;
- generators;
- medical stations;
- trader stations;
- barracks;
- defensive positions.

Construction affects:

- local defense;
- AI navigation;
- settlement efficiency;
- caravan survivability;
- production output.

---

# Economy Philosophy

The economy is systemic and physically grounded.

Resources exist as:

- physical items;
- stored materials;
- transported cargo.

The economy operates through:

- production chains;
- logistics;
- supply and demand;
- storage;
- resource scarcity.

Example production chain:

Metal Scrap → Processed Metal → Casings → Ammunition

Infrastructure requires:

- electricity;
- fuel;
- manpower;
- maintenance.

---

# Population Philosophy

Population is one of the most valuable strategic resources.

NPCs:

- can die permanently;
- can desert;
- can switch factions;
- can become spies;
- can lose morale;
- can rebel.

Specialists matter:

- engineers;
- medics;
- traders;
- scientists;
- fighters.

The loss of experienced personnel should create meaningful consequences.

---

# Faction Philosophy

Factions are autonomous strategic actors.

Each faction:

- expands;
- manages territory;
- protects logistics;
- reacts to threats;
- negotiates;
- wages war;
- develops economically.

Faction relationships can change dynamically.

Possible states:

- alliance;
- neutrality;
- hostility.

---

# Tone And Atmosphere

Zone Frontier does not attempt to change the core tone of STALKER.

The Zone remains:

- hostile;
- dangerous;
- unstable;
- morally ambiguous.

The mod introduces:

- systemic political behavior;
- emergent faction dynamics;
- long-term strategic progression.

The Zone does not become civilized.

It becomes contested.

---

# Technical Direction

## Platform

Primary target:

- S.T.A.L.K.E.R. GAMMA 0.9.5

The project is designed as:

- a modular MO2-compatible mod;
- loaded after GAMMA;
- heavily integrated into existing systems.

---

## Technical Constraints

The project intentionally avoids:

- standalone engine forks;
- custom executables;
- deep renderer rewrites.

The project prioritizes:

- script-layer integration;
- data-driven systems;
- performance on weaker hardware;
- compatibility with GAMMA updates.

---

## Existing Systems To Reuse

Zone Frontier aims to reuse:

- Warfare ownership systems;
- ALife simulation;
- squads;
- existing economy;
- existing items;
- existing crafting systems;
- existing NPC systems;
- existing construction/decor placement framework.

---

# MVP Direction

The initial MVP focuses on:

- strategic resource points;
- logistics;
- caravans;
- faction economy;
- outpost control;
- basic tactical construction;
- strategic AI behavior.

The MVP intentionally deprioritizes:

- decorative construction;
- advanced politics;
- advanced diplomacy;
- advanced espionage;
- fully simulated civilian life.

---

# Design Philosophy

Zone Frontier is designed around:

- emergent gameplay;
- replayability;
- long-term progression;
- systemic interaction;
- meaningful strategic decisions.

The player should constantly face:

- difficult tradeoffs;
- logistical pressure;
- manpower shortages;
- political consequences;
- dynamic world reactions.

The objective is to create:

> "A living strategic sandbox inside the Zone."
